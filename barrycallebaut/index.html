Executive thesis

A finite five-year project: unlock dislocated cash, rerate the multiple

Cocoa inflation stored cash in inventory. The plan releases that cash, cuts structural cost, and reweights the mix to gourmet chocolate.

Entry

Market dislocation

Record cocoa costs tripled net debt to CHF 3.8B and compressed the valuation to an 8.5x entry multiple on CHF 775M of operating EBITDA. The industrial engine, one in four chocolate products worldwide, is intact.

Hold

Operational catalysts

Inventory normalization releases CHF 1.5B+. The BC Next Level program delivers CHF 250M of recurring annual savings. Contract reindexing and gourmet mix add a further CHF 175M of EBITDA.

Exit

Value harvest

Exit horizon

Engineered for a Year-5 trade sale to a strategic or long-duration buyer

Every successful buyout begins with the exit: who pays the premium, why, and what must be true by then.

Buyer pools and acquisition logic

Confectionery strategics (Mondelez, Ferrero, Hershey): replicating 66 certified plants and the liquid chocolate network would take billions and a decade of approvals. Strategics pay for certainty of outsourced supply.

Infrastructure and sovereign capital seeks bond-like, contractually indexed B2B cash generation with utility characteristics.

Three non-negotiable exit milestones

  1. Balance sheet: net debt down to 1.1x EBITDA from 4.9x at peak.
  2. Contract design: industrial volume on rolling cost indexation with resets no slower than 30 days.
  3. Margin quality: Gourmet and Specialties at 35% of gross profit.

Strategic perimeter

Retain integrated cocoa processing: the carve-out destroys more than it fixes

Two thirds of Global Cocoa output feeds our own factories. The perimeter decision is about supply security and margin capture.

Whole-company buyout Recommended

  • Internal supply security: two thirds of grinding feeds chocolate plants, zero transfer friction.
  • Customer trust: farm-to-factory traceability and certification for Nestle, Unilever and other Tier-1 accounts.
  • Margin capture: internal cocoa liquor and butter avoid third-party trading spreads.
  • Commodity risk solved financially: rolling hedges plus contractual pass-through, without a corporate divorce.

Cocoa carve-out Rejected

  • 18 to 24 months of separation friction and transfer pricing disputes.
  • Counterparty supply risk on two thirds of internal volume.
  • Loss of integrated scale compresses the exit multiple.
  • Locks in the commodity narrative exactly when the turnaround needs a quality narrative.

Distress management

Four weekly tripwires, each with a pre-approved playbook

Early detection beats negotiation. Every tripwire triggers an action that is already rehearsed.

T1

13-week cash variance above 5%

Freeze non-safety capex, renegotiate supplier terms, delay non-contractual outflows. Weekly review with the sponsor operating committee.

T2

Pass-through lag beyond 30 days

Enforce 30-day indexation resets; temporarily halt shipments on non-indexed accounts. Bi-weekly commercial pricing audit.

T3

Line utilization below 75%

Consolidate shift patterns within 14 days and transfer volume to regional automated plants. Monthly plant review.

T4

Gourmet gross margin below 32%

SKU profitability audit; discounting on artisanal lines stops immediately. Monthly divisional review.
